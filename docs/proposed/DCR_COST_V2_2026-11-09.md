# DCR-Cost v2 (deadline-split candidates) and the first comparison with cost-minimization baselines — 9 November 2026

Status: development results on the expansion sample. **Not frozen.** The unseen and holdout sets have not been touched.

## What changed from v1 (`DCRCostImprovedScheduler`) to v2 (`DCRCostSplitScheduler`)

1. **Wider candidate set.** v1 had the bound-rule plans for γ ∈ {1, 5/4, 3/2, 2, 3}, plus the DCR-v1 Admission
   guardrail when none of them meets D. v2 adds three deterministic deadline-distribution plans
   (`algorithms/dcr_cost/split.py`), one for each communication weight λ ∈ {0, 1/2, 1}. This is the classical
   sub-deadline rule of Yu et al. 2005, IC-PCP and ProLiS: `sd_i = D·(R − rank_i + fast_i)/R`, then the cheapest
   resource meeting `sd_i`, else the earliest finish. λ replaces ProLiS's random edge inclusion with a fixed
   grid, so the method stays deterministic. Both grids are predeclared, not tuned.
2. **Every feasible candidate is improved.** v1 improved only the plan that was cheapest *before* improvement.
   v2 runs the downgrade pass on every candidate whose exact rebuild meets D, and keeps the cheapest result.
3. **Dominance.** v2's candidates include v1's: the guardrail is added in exactly the case where v1 returns
   it. So v2 never costs more than v1. Checked on all 594 instances: 0 cases where v2 is costlier.
4. **Faster improvement pass, identical results.** Each trial move re-simulates only from the moved task's
   position in the order; the earlier tasks never depend on later ones. The output is identical to the full
   rebuild: 0 mismatches over 900 random prefix resumes and 30 full improvement runs (60 and 400 tasks), at
   2–3× the speed.

A defect found and fixed during this step: the first v2 build dropped the guardrail whenever a split
candidate was feasible. It cost more than v1 on 5 tight instances, by 0.7–6.7%. The 50 instances where v1 uses
the guardrail were re-run with the fix. Every other instance takes an unchanged code path.

## Baselines

ProLiS (MAJOR adaptation) and CMaS (MINOR adaptation). The audits are in `docs/paper_audits/`. ProLiS was run
with 10 seeds (0–9), the paper's protocol, and is reported three ways: seed 0, the mean over seeds that meet D,
and the best of 10. The best of 10 gives ProLiS ten times the work, as an upper reference. CMaS is
deterministic; why it misses every tight and moderate deadline is diagnosed in `CMAS_2017.md`.

## Reading

- v2 meets D on 594/594. Against every ProLiS protocol it wins 88–92% of instances and loses 1–4%. The
  median saving over ProLiS is 1.3–1.7%, the mean 11–12%: most instances are close, and a minority show large
  gaps, largest on relaxed deadlines (IQR down to 0.667).
- On the 60-task instances, v2 is 2.7% above the best CP-SAT cost (median), against 7.9% for v1 and 11–12% for
  ProLiS. The p90 falls from 1.74 to 1.30.
- Where CP-SAT proved optimality (67 instances), v2 is within 1.3% median (v1 also 1.013; ProLiS 1.091).
- Remaining weakness: runtime. v2 runs up to 9 improvement passes; large instances take tens of seconds.
